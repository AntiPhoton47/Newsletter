Upcoming Investment Opportunities

Need To Know

Ovaries may keep working after menopause, just not at the old job

Source: Nature Briefing

Nature Briefing's June 26 lead points to a shift in how biologists may need to think about menopause. The core claim is not that ovaries stay reproductive. It is that they may take on an immune role after egg production stops. Menopause is often described as the end of ovarian function. The research instead suggests a change of function.

If that result holds up, it changes more than terminology. It could affect how researchers think about aging, inflammation, endocrine signaling, and the way reproductive organs interact with the rest of the body after fertility ends. The useful lesson is methodological: organs often look obsolete only when the question being asked is too narrow.

Read source at nature.com

NASA's satellite-rescue mission turns orbital housekeeping into a systems test

Source: Nature Briefing

NASA's planned satellite-rescue mission sounds modest until you look at what it implies. The agency intends to lift the orbit of a falling satellite by about 200 kilometers. That makes the mission a concrete answer to a broad orbital problem: a mature space economy cannot treat maintenance, debris risk, and mission extension as separate categories forever.

The interesting part is not spectacle. It is normalization. Once satellite servicing becomes routine enough to appear in an ordinary science briefing, low-Earth orbit starts to look less like a domain of one-shot heroic missions and more like industrial infrastructure with repair, towing, and life-extension needs.
